# Portfolio Holdings

The **Portfolio Holdings** page provides a detailed view of individual investments and their performance.

It is designed to answer three basic questions:

1. **What investments do I own?**
2. **How much have I invested and received back from each investment?**
3. **How well has each investment performed?**

The page can also display investments that have already been fully exited, allowing historical investment results to be reviewed when needed.

---

## Understanding the terminology

Some terms on this page have a specific meaning within the Personal Finance Tracker.

### Yield

In this report, **Yield** means cash that has been returned by the investment.

It includes cash received through:

- sale proceeds
- dividends
- interest
- other investment income

This is intentionally broader than the conventional financial definition of *dividend yield*.

For example, if $10,000 was invested and the investment subsequently paid a $500 dividend, the $500 contributes to **Yield**.

If part of the investment was later sold for $3,000, those sale proceeds also contribute to Yield.

> **Important:** On this report, Yield means **cash returned from the investment**, not simply dividend yield.

### Unrealized

**Unrealized** represents the gain or loss still contained in the current holding.

Conceptually:

**Unrealized Gain = Current Market Value − Investment Cost**

A positive value means the remaining investment is currently worth more than the associated investment cost.

A negative value means it is currently worth less.

### Return

**Return** represents the total dollar gain or loss generated by the investment.

Conceptually:

**Return = Yield + Unrealized Gain**

This combines:

- cash already received from the investment; and
- gain or loss still contained in the current market value.

This distinction is important.

**Yield** tells you what has already come back from the investment.

**Unrealized** tells you what gain or loss remains in the investment.

**Return** brings both together.

# Holdings table

Each row represents an investment identified by its symbol.

## Symbol

The ticker, fund code, or other identifying symbol assigned to the investment.

Examples may include publicly traded stock symbols, exchange-traded fund symbols, or mutual-fund codes.

## Name

The full name of the investment.

## Price

The price currently being used to value one unit of the investment.

The value depends on the selected **Price Source**.

When **Live** is selected, the model uses the available current market price. Where a live price is unavailable, the model can fall back to the latest available portfolio snapshot price.

## Qty

The number of units currently held.

For active investments, this represents the remaining quantity owned.

A fully exited investment will normally have no remaining quantity.

## Invested

**Invested** represents the cumulative purchase cost attributed to the investment.

It includes investment purchases and applicable investment fees.

Importantly, reinvested investment income is treated as a new investment.

For example:

1. You originally invest **$10,000**.
2. The investment generates a **$1,000 dividend**.
3. That $1,000 is recorded as Yield because the investment generated the income.
4. If the $1,000 is reinvested, it also becomes an additional investment purchase.

The cumulative amount invested therefore becomes **$11,000**.

This treatment allows the report to preserve both parts of the story:

- the investment successfully generated $1,000 of income; and
- that income was subsequently invested again.

## Yield

Yield shows the cumulative cash returned by the investment.

It may include:

- dividends
- interest
- investment income
- proceeds from units or shares sold

Because sale proceeds are included, an investment that has been fully exited can have a large Yield even though its current market value is zero.

### Example

Suppose:

- Total Invested = **$10,000**
- Sale proceeds received = **$7,000**
- Dividends received = **$1,000**
- Remaining Market Value = **$4,000**

Yield would be:

**$7,000 + $1,000 = $8,000**

The investment has therefore returned $8,000 of cash while still retaining $4,000 of market value.

## Unrealized

Unrealized shows the gain or loss associated with the remaining market value of the investment.

If an investment has been fully exited, its remaining market value is zero and its unrealized amount reflects that there is no remaining holding.

The final investment result is therefore better understood using **Return** and **ROI** rather than looking at Unrealized alone.

## Mkt. Value

**Market Value** represents the estimated current value of the remaining investment:

**Quantity Held × Selected Price**

A fully exited investment has no remaining market value.

## Return

Return shows the investment's total dollar gain or loss.

Conceptually:

**Return = Yield + Unrealized Gain**

It therefore captures both realized and unrealized components of investment performance.

### Example

Suppose:

- Invested = **$10,000**
- Yield = **$1,000**
- Market Value = **$12,000**

The unrealized gain is:

**$12,000 − $10,000 = $2,000**

Total Return is therefore:

**$1,000 + $2,000 = $3,000**

The investment has generated $3,000 of total economic gain.

# ROI

**Return on Investment (ROI)** expresses the cumulative Return as a percentage of cumulative Invested cost.

Conceptually:

**ROI = Total Return ÷ Invested**

Using the previous example:

- Invested = $10,000
- Return = $3,000

ROI is:

**$3,000 ÷ $10,000 = 30%**

ROI is a **cumulative measure**.

It does not consider how long the investment has been held.

---

## Reinvested income and ROI

Reinvested income deserves special attention when interpreting ROI.

Suppose:

- Original investment = **$10,000**
- Dividend generated = **$1,000**

The investment has successfully generated $1,000 of Yield.

If the dividend is then reinvested, cumulative Invested becomes:

**$10,000 + $1,000 = $11,000**

The additional investment increases the denominator of the ROI calculation.

As a result, the displayed ROI may decrease after reinvestment even though the investment successfully generated additional income.

This is intentional.

The page preserves the full picture through separate measures:

- **Yield** shows the income or cash the investment has already generated.
- **Invested** shows how much capital has ultimately been committed, including reinvestments.
- **Unrealized** shows the gain or loss still contained in the holding.
- **Return** combines Yield and Unrealized performance.
- **ROI** compares total Return with cumulative Invested capital.

Therefore, ROI should be interpreted together with Yield and Return rather than in isolation.

---

# Ann. Return

**Ann. Return** represents the investment's annualized return.

Unlike ROI, annualized return considers **when the investment cash flows occurred**.

The calculation uses the timing of investment purchases, sales, income and the current ending value to calculate an annualized rate.

This makes it more useful when comparing investments held for different lengths of time.

For example:

- Investment A earns 20% over one year.
- Investment B earns 20% over four years.

Both have the same cumulative ROI, but Investment A produced that return much faster.

Their annualized returns will therefore be significantly different.

### ROI vs Annualized Return

Use **ROI** when asking:

> How much money has this investment made relative to the amount invested?

Use **Ann. Return** when asking:

> How quickly has this investment generated its return?

Both measures provide useful but different information.

# Active and exited investments

The **Holding Status** filter allows investments to be viewed according to whether they are still owned.

## Active

An investment is considered **Active** when units remain in the portfolio.

Use this view when evaluating the performance and value of the **current portfolio**.

## Exited

An investment is considered **Exited** when the remaining quantity is effectively zero.

Exited investments remain useful for historical analysis because their realized investment results are preserved.

For an exited investment:

- Qty is zero
- Market Value is zero
- Yield can include sale proceeds and income received
- Return shows the final economic gain or loss
- ROI shows the cumulative historical result

## All

Displays both active and exited investments.

This is useful when reviewing the complete investment history rather than only the portfolio that currently exists.

---

# Price Source

The **Price Source** filter controls which price is used to value active holdings.

## Live

Uses the current available market price where possible.

If a live market price is unavailable for a particular investment, the model uses the available portfolio snapshot price as a fallback.

## AceMoney

Uses the price stored in the portfolio snapshot.

This allows current calculated valuations to be compared with the portfolio values recorded in the source system.

---

# Other filters

## Account Name

Limits the table to investments associated with selected investment accounts.

## Financial Institution

Limits the analysis to investments held through a particular financial institution.

## Owner

Filters investments according to the assigned account owner.

## Purpose

Filters investments according to the financial purpose assigned to their account.

This can help distinguish investments intended for purposes such as retirement, education, home purchase, emergency savings, or other financial goals.

## Gain / Loss

Allows investments to be narrowed according to whether their investment performance represents a gain or loss.

This is useful for quickly isolating investments that may require further review.

## Show Top/Bottom 10

Provides a faster way to focus on the strongest or weakest investment results.

Options include:

- **All**
- **Top 10**
- **Worst 10**

The ranking is based on the investment's ROI within the current filter context.

# Reading an active investment

For an active investment, consider the columns together.

For example:

| Measure | Example |
|---|---:|
| Invested | $20,000 |
| Yield | $4,000 |
| Market Value | $30,000 |
| Unrealized | $10,000 |
| Return | $14,000 |
| ROI | 70% |

The interpretation is:

- $20,000 has been invested cumulatively.
- $4,000 has already been returned through income and/or sales.
- The remaining holding is worth $30,000.
- The remaining holding contains $10,000 of unrealized gain.
- Total economic gain is $14,000.
- The investment has generated cumulative value equivalent to 70% of the amount invested.

The annualized return then provides additional context by considering how long it took to generate that result.

---

# Reading an exited investment

Suppose an investment shows:

| Measure | Example |
|---|---:|
| Invested | $10,000 |
| Yield | $12,000 |
| Market Value | $0 |
| Return | $2,000 |
| ROI | 20% |

The investment is no longer owned.

However, the historical result remains visible:

- $10,000 was invested.
- $12,000 ultimately came back through sale proceeds and investment income.
- Nothing remains invested.
- The investment generated a final $2,000 gain.
- Its cumulative ROI was 20%.

This is why exited investments can still be useful for historical performance analysis even though they are not part of the current portfolio.

---

# Total row

The total row summarizes the holdings currently included by the page filters.

It should be interpreted as a **portfolio-level calculation**, not as an average of the percentages shown for individual investments.

For example, total ROI is calculated using aggregate portfolio gain relative to aggregate investment cost.

Individual ROI percentages are **not simply averaged together**.

This distinction matters because larger investments have a greater economic impact on total portfolio performance than very small investments.

---

# Practical uses

The Portfolio Holdings page can be used to:

- see all current investments in one place
- review cumulative investment performance
- compare cumulative ROI with annualized return
- identify investments with gains or losses
- understand how much cash an investment has already returned
- distinguish realized cash returns from unrealized gains
- review investments that have already been sold
- compare current market valuation with portfolio snapshot pricing
- analyze investments by account, institution, owner, or financial purpose
- isolate the strongest and weakest historical performers

For deeper analysis of portfolio concentration, holding age, price trend and review indicators, use the **Portfolio Analysis** page.
