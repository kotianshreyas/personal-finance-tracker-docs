# Portfolio Holdings

The **Portfolio Holdings** page brings your investments together so you can see what you own, how much they are worth, and how they have performed.

Use this page to:

- review individual investments;
- compare money invested with money received back;
- understand gains and losses;
- distinguish investments still owned from those already sold; and
- open a detailed breakdown of a particular investment.

## Reading the holdings table

Each row represents an investment. If you own the same investment in several accounts, its overall results can be reviewed together here.

### Symbol and Name

**Symbol** is the investment’s short identifier, such as a stock ticker or fund code.

**Name** identifies the investment in full.

### Price

Price shows the value being used for one unit of the investment.

The selected **Price Source** affects this value:

- **Live** uses an available market price. If one is unavailable, the report may use the price recorded in your portfolio snapshot.
- **AceMoney** uses the price recorded in your portfolio snapshot.

“Live” does not necessarily mean a continuously updating price. Some investments, particularly mutual funds, may use an older recorded price.

### Qty

Qty shows how many units you still own.

For example, buying 100 units and later selling 40 leaves a quantity of 60.

An investment that has been fully sold normally has no remaining quantity.

### Invested

Invested shows the accumulated amount spent buying the investment, including applicable investment fees.

It is different from the investment’s current value.

Income that is reinvested purchases additional units and therefore increases the amount invested.

For example:

- You buy $10,000 of an investment.
- It generates $500 of income.
- You reinvest that $500.

The accumulated amount invested becomes $10,500. The $500 is also part of the income generated by the investment.

### Yield

On this page, **Yield means money received back from the investment**.

It can include:

- proceeds from selling units;
- dividends;
- interest; and
- other investment income.

This is an amount of money, rather than a dividend-yield percentage.

**Yield is not the same as profit.** Sale proceeds include the recovery of money you originally invested.

For example, selling an investment for $1,200 after buying it for $1,000 returns $1,200 of cash, but the profit is $200.

### Mkt. Value

Market Value shows the estimated value of the units you still own.

**Market Value = Units Owned × Price**

For example, 60 units valued at $12 each have a market value of $720.

This is an estimated holding value, rather than a guaranteed amount you would receive after selling costs or price changes.

### Unrealized

Unrealized shows the gain or loss on the units you still own.

**Unrealized Gain or Loss = Current Value − Cost of the Remaining Units**

For example, if the remaining units cost $600 and are now worth $720, the unrealized gain is $120.

This gain is called unrealized because you still own those units.

### Return

Return shows the total dollar gain or loss generated by the investment.

It brings together:

- profit or loss on units already sold;
- gain or loss on units still owned; and
- investment income received.

**Return = Gain or Loss on Sold Units + Gain or Loss on Remaining Units + Investment Income**

It does not treat all sale proceeds as profit.

#### Example: selling part of an investment

Suppose you:

- buy 100 units for $1,000;
- sell 40 units for $480;
- receive $50 of investment income; and
- still own 60 units worth $720.

Assuming each unit originally cost $10 and ignoring fees:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Profit on the 40 units sold | $80 |
| Gain on the 60 units still owned | $120 |
| Investment income | $50 |
| **Total Return** | **$250** |

You have received $530 in cash from the sale and income, but part of that cash is your original investment being returned.

### ROI

**Return on Investment (ROI)** expresses the total gain or loss as a percentage of the amount invested.

**ROI = Total Return ÷ Investment Cost**

For example, a $250 return on a $1,000 investment represents a 25% ROI.

ROI is cumulative. It does not tell you how quickly the return was earned.

A 25% return over one year and a 25% return over five years have the same cumulative percentage, but very different investment outcomes.

### Ann. Return

Annualized Return expresses performance as a yearly rate.

It considers both:

- how much money went into or came out of the investment; and
- when those movements occurred.

This helps compare investments held for different lengths of time.

For example, earning 20% over one year is different from earning 20% over four years. Annualized Return helps put those results on a comparable time basis.

For investments held only briefly, the yearly equivalent can look unusually high or low. Read it together with the holding period and dollar return.

A blank annualized return means a usable result is unavailable. It does not mean the investment earned zero.

## Investments still owned and investments already sold

Use **Holding Status** to focus on the investments you want to review.

### Active

Active investments still have units remaining.

Use this view when reviewing your current portfolio.

### Exited

Exited investments have been fully sold.

Their market value may be zero, but their sale proceeds, income, and investment results remain useful for reviewing past decisions.

An annualized return may not be available for every exited investment.

## How to use the page

### Understand an investment’s overall result

Read **Invested**, **Yield**, **Market Value**, and **Return** together.

These tell you:

- how much was committed;
- how much came back;
- how much remains invested; and
- how much was gained or lost overall.

### Compare performance

Use ROI to compare cumulative results.

Then check Annualized Return and the holding period to understand how quickly those results were earned.

### Review a partial sale

A sale reduces the units still owned and produces cash proceeds.

Look at both Return and remaining Market Value to understand the complete result. Cash received from a sale should not be interpreted entirely as profit.

### Investigate an individual investment

Where available:

1. Right-click an investment.
2. Select **Drill through**.
3. Choose **Holding Breakdown**.

The detail page opens with that investment selected.

## Before comparing figures

Keep the selected accounts, filters, and price source consistent.

Also check the dates shown in the report. Transaction information and investment prices may have different update dates.

For a broader comparison of position size, performance, and recent price behaviour, use **Portfolio Analysis**.
