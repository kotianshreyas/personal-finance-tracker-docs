# Balance Sheet

The **Balance Sheet** page provides a view of financial position: what is owned, what is owed, and the resulting net worth.

It is useful for answering:

1. What assets are included in the report?
2. How much debt is associated with the selected accounts?
3. What is the difference between those assets and liabilities?
4. How is net worth changing over time?
5. How much progress has been made toward major financial goals?
6. Is the current liquidity and investment risk position close to the intended targets?

## True Net Worth

The top-left section shows **True Net Worth**.

In simple terms:

**Net Worth = Assets − Liabilities**

The page also shows the change from the previous month.

A positive change means the selected net worth is higher than it was in the previous month.

A negative change means it is lower.

## Assets

Assets represent items with positive financial value.

Depending on the accounts tracked, these can include:

- bank balances;
- cash;
- savings;
- investments;
- fixed deposits;
- other recorded assets.

An asset balance is a value at a point in time.

It is different from income received during a period.

For example, a savings balance of **$15,000** describes money held, while monthly income of **$5,000** describes financial activity during a month.

## Liabilities

Liabilities represent amounts owed.

These can include:

- credit-card balances;
- lines of credit;
- loans;
- other recorded obligations.

Liabilities in the report can appear as negative values or in brackets.

When a liability is already displayed as a negative balance, it should not be subtracted again when mentally interpreting net worth.

## Net worth example

Suppose the selected accounts contain:

| Component | Example |
|---|---:|
| Cash and savings | $25,000 |
| Investments | $175,000 |
| Other recorded assets | $100,000 |
| Total assets | $300,000 |
| Total liabilities, expressed as amounts owed | $120,000 |
| Net worth | $180,000 |

The result describes financial position.

It does **not** mean that $180,000 is immediately available to spend.

Some assets may be:

- invested;
- reserved for particular goals;
- difficult to convert immediately to cash.

## Account table

The left side groups financial accounts into major categories.

Examples include:

- **A. Credit Cards**
- **B. Bank accounts**
- **C. Cash Accounts**
- **D. Short Term Accounts**
- **E. Securities**
- **F. Fixed Deposits**
- **G. Advances Amortization**
- **H. Loans**

Groups can be expanded to view individual institutions and accounts.

## Available (CAD)

For relevant credit accounts, **Available (CAD)** shows the remaining credit available after considering the balance and recorded limit.

This should not be treated as an asset or additional wealth.

Available credit is borrowing capacity.

## Balance (USD)

Shows the native US-dollar balance for applicable USD accounts.

## Balance (CAD)

Shows account values expressed in Canadian dollars.

This allows USD and CAD financial positions to be considered together.

## Financial goals

The upper-right table compares selected financial goals with their current progress.

Examples shown in the report include:

- Emergency Fund;
- Home Buying;
- Closing Costs;
- Education Fund;
- Retirement Fund;
- RRSP;
- FHSA.

### Goal

The target amount.

### Current

The amount currently associated with the goal.

### Progress

The percentage of the target currently achieved.

Progress bars make it easier to identify goals that are:

- fully funded;
- approaching their target;
- still requiring significant additional savings.

## Emergency Fund

The Emergency Fund goal tracks money intended to cover unexpected expenses or temporary income disruption.

A fully funded emergency reserve can appear as 100% progress.

## Home Buying

Shows progress toward the amount assigned to the home-buying goal.

This is separate from closing costs.

## Closing Costs

Tracks money reserved specifically for expenses associated with completing a home purchase.

Keeping closing costs separate helps avoid assuming that every dollar of the down-payment fund is available for the purchase price itself.

## Education Fund

Shows the amount associated with education savings.

## Retirement Fund

Shows progress toward the longer-term retirement target.

Because retirement targets are usually much larger and longer term, a relatively low percentage does not necessarily mean the plan is failing. The percentage should be considered together with age, time horizon, future contributions, and investment growth.

## RRSP and FHSA goals

These rows show progress toward the selected targets for the registered plans represented in the report.

They should not be confused with official government contribution room.

For contribution-room monitoring, use the **Contribution Tracker** page.

## Long-Term Risk Ratio

The **Long-Term Risk Ratio** compares the current investment mix with the intended target.

For example:

- Target: **70:30**
- Current: **92:8**

A message such as **More equity than target** indicates that the portfolio currently contains more equity exposure than the stated long-term target.

The indicator is intended to highlight allocation differences, not automatically recommend a transaction.

## Liquid Ratio

The **Liquid Ratio** compares readily available financial resources with the selected liquidity target.

A result below target means accessible resources are lower than the intended level.

This can matter when planning for:

- emergencies;
- large near-term expenses;
- home purchases;
- other short-term needs.

## Net Worth Over Time

The chart at the bottom-right shows how net worth has changed historically.

The longer-term direction is usually more informative than any single monthly movement.

## Reading changes over time

A change in net worth can have several causes.

### Investment values

A higher investment valuation can increase net worth without creating immediately spendable cash.

### Transfers

Moving money between two accounts that are both included in the report changes where the money is held but does not, by itself, create wealth.

### Debt repayment

Debt repayment generally:

- uses cash;
- reduces a liability.

Its effect is different from earning income.

### Income and expenses

Income can increase financial resources while expenses reduce them, but the effect on net worth also depends on what happens to the resulting cash.

## Dates and account coverage

Check the balance date before relying on the total.

A balance sheet describes financial position at a point in time.

It is different from Income & Expenses, which describes activity over a period.

The page reflects only the accounts represented in the report and the filters currently selected.

If the Owner filter is changed, the resulting net worth may describe only one person's selected accounts rather than the entire household.

## Valuation timing

Not every balance necessarily changes at exactly the same time.

For example:

- investment prices may be refreshed from market information;
- bank balances change through recorded transactions;
- some manually maintained financial values may update less frequently.

Small timing differences should therefore be considered when interpreting a very recent balance sheet.

## Filters

### Owner

Shows the balance sheet for all owners or a selected owner.

### Price Source

Controls the available investment valuation source.

### Date

Allows a selected financial position to be reviewed.

**Latest** shows the most recent available transaction period.

## Practical uses

- Review the balance between assets and debt.
- Monitor True Net Worth.
- Compare financial position using consistent dates and account scope.
- Review progress toward major goals.
- Check liquidity against its target.
- Review whether the long-term investment allocation differs from its intended risk mix.
- Distinguish overall net worth from available cash.

## Relationship with other pages

Use **Cash Flow** to understand cash movements and projected cash availability.

Use **Income & Expenses** to investigate income and spending over a period.

Use **Investment Overview**, **Portfolio Holdings**, and **Portfolio Analysis** to examine investment assets in more detail.

Use **Contribution Tracker** to monitor registered-account contribution activity and room.

## Key question answered

> **What is my overall financial position, and is it moving in the direction I intend?**
