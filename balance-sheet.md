# Balance Sheet

The **Balance Sheet** page provides a view of financial position: what is owned, what is owed, and the resulting net worth.

It is useful for answering:

1. What assets are included in the report?
2. How much debt is associated with the selected accounts?
3. What is the difference between those assets and liabilities?

## Assets

Assets represent items with positive financial value. Depending on the accounts tracked, these can include cash, savings, investments, and other recorded assets.

An asset balance is a value at a point in time. It is different from income received during a period.

For example, a savings balance of **$15,000** describes money held; monthly income of **$5,000** describes activity over a month.

## Liabilities

Liabilities represent amounts owed, such as tracked credit-card balances, loans, or mortgages.

Check how debt is displayed before interpreting the total. A report can show liabilities as positive amounts owed or as negative balances.

When liabilities are shown as positive amounts owed:

**Net Worth = Assets − Liabilities**

When liabilities are already negative, adding their signed balances to assets gives the equivalent result. Do not subtract the same debt twice.

## Net worth example

Suppose the selected accounts contain:

| Component | Example |
|---|---:|
| Cash and savings | $25,000 |
| Investments | $175,000 |
| Other recorded assets | $100,000 |
| Total assets | $300,000 |
| Total liabilities, expressed as amounts owed | $120,000 |
| Net worth | $180,000 |

The result describes the selected financial position. It does not mean that $180,000 is immediately available to spend: some assets may be invested or difficult to convert to cash.

## Reading changes over time

When comparing balances across dates, consider the underlying reason for a change.

A higher investment valuation can increase net worth without generating spendable cash. A transfer between two included bank accounts changes where money is held but does not, by itself, create wealth.

Debt repayment reduces a liability and uses cash. Its effect differs from earning income or recording an investment gain.

## Dates and account coverage

Check the balance date and included accounts before relying on the total. A balance sheet reflects only the assets and liabilities represented in its source data.

If an account is excluded by a filter, its balance no longer contributes to the selected view. A filtered net-worth figure therefore may describe only one owner, institution, or purpose rather than the entire household.

Valuations can also differ in freshness; an investment price and a manually recorded asset balance may refer to different dates.

## Relationship with other pages

Use **Cash Flow** to understand movements and projected cash availability. Use **Income Expense** to investigate income and spending over a period. Use the portfolio pages to examine investment valuation and performance.

## Practical uses

- Review the balance between assets and debt.
- Compare financial position using consistent dates and account scope.
- Identify missing or outdated balances for review.
- Distinguish overall net worth from available cash.
